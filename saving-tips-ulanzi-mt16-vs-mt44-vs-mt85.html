The names are easy to mix up: the MT-44 and the MT-44B are different products. The MT-44 is a simple extendable selfie stick with three legs. The MT-44B adds a remote, a fourth leg, a cold shoe for a light and a quick release plate, for four cents more.

Ulanzi MT-16: the desk and travel mini tripod

The MT-16 is the smallest of the four: 22 cm closed, 44 cm at full stretch and 170 g. Ulanzi lists it as a 2-in-1 tripod and selfie stick for a phone or an action camera, with a ball head on a standard 1/4"-20 screw and a 1.5 kg maximum load. It's made of ABS and aluminum.

At 44 cm it won't hold a phone at eye height when you stand, so it suits desk calls, tabletop video and travel more than full-body shots. It's $23.95, or $21.56 with the code; Ulanzi also sells two for $45.50.

Ulanzi MT-44: the cheapest tall selfie stick

The MT-44 extends in six sections to 146.5 cm, so it works as a selfie stick for group photos and as a short stand for live streams. Its phone holder folds into the top and opens to clamp a phone, and Ulanzi says it also takes mirrorless and action cameras and lights. Its product page doesn't list the weight, the folded length or a remote.

It's $25.95, or $23.36 with the code, and rated 4.9 out of 5 from 54 reviews on Ulanzi's store, the highest of the four.

See the MT-44 at Ulanzi

Ulanzi MT-44B: the MT-44 with a remote and auto legs

The MT-44B is a seven-section selfie stick that reaches 146.5 cm, or 150 cm standing on its four legs. Tap the base on the ground and the legs open by themselves, which Ulanzi points out for people with long nails. The wireless remote slides out of the handle and reaches 10 m. The phone clamp has a cold shoe for a small light, and the ball head turns 360°.

It weighs about 415 g, the heaviest of the four, and is about 33.5 cm folded. It's $25.99, or $23.39 with the code, and Ulanzi sells it with a VL49 RGB light for $44.99.

See the MT-44B at Ulanzi

Ulanzi MT85: for MagSafe phones and quick setups

The MT85's phone mount is magnetic on both sides: Ulanzi lists 20 N of hold for a phone and 25 N for an accessory on the other side, so a MagSafe iPhone or a phone with a magnetic case snaps on without a clamp. Set it down and its four legs open on their own. It extends in seven sections to 150 cm, comes with a Bluetooth remote with a 10 m range, and weighs about 385 g.

The trade-offs: its load limit is 1 kg on the center column, lower than the MT-16 and MT-44B, and it's the most expensive at $32.99 ($29.69 with the code). Its rating, 4.7 out of 5 from 67 reviews, is the lowest of the four. Ulanzi sells it with a ring light for $51.99, or with a ring light and a microphone for $89.99.

Questions people ask

Which Ulanzi tripod is best for a phone?

It depends on how you film. For a desk or table, the MT-16 ($23.95) is the smallest. For a tall selfie stick with a remote, the MT-44B ($25.99). For a MagSafe iPhone, the MT85 ($32.99) holds the phone with magnets.

Ulanzi MT-44 vs MT-44B: what's the difference?

The MT-44 ($25.95) is a six-section selfie stick with three legs and a folding phone clamp. The MT-44B ($25.99) has seven sections, four legs that open by themselves, a 10 m remote, a cold shoe and a ball head with quick release.

Is the Ulanzi MT85 MagSafe?

Its mount is magnetic and Ulanzi lists it as a MagSafe tripod, with 20 N of magnetic hold for a phone and 25 N for an accessory on the other side.

How tall is the Ulanzi MT-44?

Ulanzi lists the full length of the MT-44 at 146.5 cm (57.6 in), in six sections. The MT-44B reaches 146.5 cm as a selfie stick and 150 cm standing on its legs.

Does the Ulanzi MT-16 work with a camera?

Ulanzi lists the MT-16 for phones and action cameras, with a standard 1/4"-20 screw and a 1.5 kg maximum load. It reaches only 44 cm, so it's a tabletop tripod.

Which Ulanzi tripods have a remote?

Of these four, the MT-44B and the MT85, each with a 10 m wireless remote. The MT-16 and MT-44 pages don't list one.

How much weight can a Ulanzi tripod hold?

Ulanzi lists 1.5 kg for the MT-16 and the MT-44B, and 1 kg on the center column of the MT85. The MT-44 page doesn't give a number.

Are Ulanzi tripods good?

On Ulanzi's own store, the MT-44 is rated 4.9 out of 5 from 54 reviews, the MT-16 4.8 from 68 and the MT85 4.7 from 67 (checked November 18, 2026). Those ratings are shown by the store itself.
