2. Weigh the warranty, not only the price

Omega's warranties differ a lot by model. The Effortless and the Pure Press list a 15-year limited warranty on their product pages. The JC4000, the Time Saving batch juicer and the High Speed juicer list 1 year (12 months).

So the $399.99 Pure Press costs about $27 a year over its warranty, while the $134.99 JC4000 is covered for one year. If you juice every day and plan to keep the machine, the longer warranty can be the cheaper choice over time.

3. Pick the type of juicer you'll actually use

Omega sells three kinds of juicer. The High Speed juicer ($109.99) is a 1,000-watt centrifugal model with a 3.3-inch chute: the cheapest and fastest. The JC4000 ($134.99) and the Pure Press ($399.99) are vertical slow juicers; the JC4000 turns at 50 to 75 RPM and has a 3-inch mouth. The two batch juicers, the Time Saving ($159.99) and the Effortless ($499.99 and up), take a whole recipe at once in a hopper; the Effortless hopper holds 2 liters (68 oz) and stops by itself after five minutes.

If you want a slow juicer on a budget, the JC4000 is the lowest price for one. If you want to load everything and walk away, the Time Saving batch juicer costs less than a third of the Effortless.

Shipping and returns

Orders ship within 48 hours on weekdays; orders placed Friday to Sunday go out the next Monday. In the 48 contiguous states, packages up to 9 pounds go by UPS SurePost (5 to 10 business days) and heavier ones by UPS Ground (5 to 7 business days), with UPS 3 Day, 2nd Day and Next Day Air as paid options. Alaska, Hawaii and Puerto Rico get UPS Ground only. Canadian orders include duties and taxes in the shipping fee. Sales tax is added in many US states.

Omega offers a 30-day return policy for US and Canadian customers. Fill out the contact form for a return authorization (RMA) number first; you pay the return shipping, and refunds go back to your original payment method. Juicers bought from another retailer are returned through that retailer.
