Choose the W702 for plain CarPlay and Android Auto

The W702 is the cheapest way into Carpuride's 7-inch range. It mirrors CarPlay or Android Auto from your phone over Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, so navigation, music and calls come from the apps you already use. Carpuride lists a 1024×600 IPS touchscreen at 1000 nits, glove-friendly touch, voice control through Siri or Google Assistant, and storage for up to a 256 GB memory card (not included).

Its dual Bluetooth is the part riders ask about most: Carpuride says it connects your phone and your helmet intercom, such as a Cardo or Sena, at the same time. It's rated IP67 and listed to work from -4°F to 140°F. It doesn't have the Pro's intercom mode, crash detection or tire pressure option.

The same listing also sells a 5-inch W502 for $269.99 and a 6-inch W619 for $279.99 if your dash has less room.

Choose the W702 Pro for intercom, SOS and TPMS

For $50 more, the W702 Pro keeps everything the W702 does and adds features aimed at touring. Its intercom mode lets a rider and passenger talk or share music up to 10 meters apart, though Carpuride notes that intercom mode and BT Trans mode can't run at the same time. An SOS mode detects a crash, starts a countdown and, if you don't respond, calls your emergency contact. It also adds a compass, a barometer for altitude and a light sensor that adjusts the screen.

The TPMS version, $359.99, adds 2 wireless sensors that replace your valve caps and show tire pressure and temperature on the screen, with low-pressure warnings. That's $20 for the sensors, cheaper than the Plus's TPMS option.

There's also a W702S Pro at $369.90: the same Pro features plus the plug-and-play BM05 bracket and a handlebar-mounted wired controller, according to Carpuride's own comparison table.

Choose the W702 Plus for apps, GPS and cameras

The W702 Plus is a different kind of device. Instead of only mirroring your phone, it runs Android 14 with an octa-core processor, 4 GB of RAM and 64 GB of storage, and you can install apps from Google Play; Carpuride names Rever, Kurviger, Calimoto, Spotify and YouTube. Its built-in GPS works without your phone and with offline maps, and it still does wireless CarPlay and Android Auto when you want them.

It's also a dash cam: front and rear 1080P cameras with a 140° view, loop recording and a 64 GB card in the box, plus a wired handlebar button to switch cameras, answer calls and lock clips. One warning from Carpuride matters before you install it: the Type-C port is for factory debugging only, the unit must be wired to the motorcycle's battery, and feeding more than 5V through that port can burn it out.

Questions people ask

What's the difference between the Carpuride W702 and W702 Pro?

Both are 7-inch, IP67 wireless CarPlay and Android Auto screens. The W702 ($289.99) covers the basics with dual Bluetooth. The W702 Pro ($339.99) adds a two-rider intercom, SOS crash detection, a compass, a barometer, a light sensor and an optional tire pressure (TPMS) kit.

Carpuride W702 Plus vs W702 Pro: which should I get?

Get the Pro if you only need CarPlay or Android Auto from your phone plus intercom and TPMS. Get the Plus ($409.99) if you want a full Android 14 system with Google Play apps, built-in GPS that works without your phone, and front and rear 1080P cameras.

Does the Carpuride W702 have built-in GPS?

The W702 and W702 Pro use your phone's maps through CarPlay or Android Auto. Carpuride lists a built-in GPS module only on the W702 Plus, which also works with offline maps.

Does the Carpuride W702 Pro have TPMS?

As an option. The W702 Pro with TPMS costs $359.99, $20 more than the plain Pro, and comes with 2 wireless sensors that show tire pressure and temperature on the screen.

Is the Carpuride W702 waterproof?

Carpuride rates the W702, W702 Pro and W702 Plus IP67. For the W702 it describes that as dust-tight and submersible to 1 meter for 30 minutes, working from -4°F to 140°F.

Does Carpuride work with Cardo or Sena?

Carpuride says the W702's dual Bluetooth connects your phone and your helmet intercom, such as Cardo or Sena, at the same time. On the W702 Pro, its intercom mode and its BT Trans mode can't run at once.

What's the difference between the W702 Pro and W702S Pro?

The W702S Pro ($369.90) has the same Pro features but comes with the plug-and-play BM05 bracket and a handlebar-mounted wired controller, which the W702 Pro doesn't have, according to Carpuride's comparison.

Can you power the W702 Plus by USB-C?

No. Carpuride warns that the W702 Plus's Type-C port is for factory debugging only and that it must be wired to the motorcycle's battery; more than 5V through that port can damage it.
