The short answer

Heights is a legitimate UK company with a well-rated main product and a 30-day money-back guarantee. It costs more than a basic multivitamin, so it's worth it mainly if you'd otherwise buy several separate supplements, and if you buy on a subscription with a code.

What Heights sells

Four core products: Vitals⁺ (a 20-nutrient multivitamin with omega-3), Biotic⁺ (a 7-strain probiotic with zinc), Magnesium⁺ (magnesium glycinate) and Thrive⁺ (a healthy-ageing formula), plus bundles and 3-packs.

Prices are set in pounds. On a subscription, Vitals⁺ is £40 a month and Magnesium⁺ £20.

Most of the feedback lives on Heights' own site, where Vitals⁺ averages 4.7 stars from over 6,600 reviews. Its Trustpilot page has only two reviews, too few to tell you much either way.

Is Heights legit?

Yes. Heights is a UK company that sells directly through its own site, ships to the UK, the US, Canada, Australia and most of Europe, and offers 30 days to return a product that isn't right for you. You can skip or cancel a subscription at any time.

Is it worth the money?

On a subscription, Vitals⁺ works out at about £1.33 a day, or about £1.10 a day on the 90-day 3-pack. If it replaces a separate multivitamin, fish oil and vitamin D, that can be fair value. If you only want one nutrient, a single-ingredient product will be cheaper.

Who it's for

People who'd rather take one well-rounded daily formula than several separate pills. Skip it if you're on a tight budget or only need one nutrient. Check with your doctor first if you're pregnant or take medication.
