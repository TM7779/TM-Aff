Price per year of warranty

Omega's warranties are the biggest difference between these juicers, and they change the math. Spread over its warranty, the Pure Press costs about $26.67 a year and the Effortless in black about $33.33 a year. The three cheaper models are covered for one year, so their whole price sits in that year: $109.99, $134.99 and $159.99.

That doesn't mean a cheaper juicer will break after a year, only that Omega promises less. If you plan to juice most days for years, the 15-year models are the ones Omega stands behind longest. If you're trying juicing for the first time, a 1-year model risks less money up front.

Effortless batch juicer: least prep, longest cover

The Effortless takes a whole recipe at once: Omega says its 2-liter (68 oz) hopper fits whole fruits and vegetables, so there's little chopping. It has an all-in-one auger and no juicing screen, which means fewer parts to wash, and a timer that stops it after five minutes, so you can walk away. It runs at 70 RPM on a 150-watt motor, weighs 13.67 lb, and has the highest store rating of the five, 4.5 from 80 reviews.

It's also the most expensive: $499.99 in black and $549.99 in white, or $449.99 and $494.99 with the code. The extra $50 for white buys only the color.

Pure Press VSJ843: the classic vertical slow juicer

The Pure Press is a vertical slow juicer with a 3-stage auger and an anti-clog function. At 43 RPM it's the slowest of the five, and Omega says it can make plant-based milk as well as juice. Its parts can go on the top rack of a dishwasher. It's the heaviest of the five at 19.8 lb, so it's a juicer that lives on the counter.

It has the most reviews, 108, but the lowest average, 4.1, so read the low-star reviews on the product page before you buy. At $399.99, or $359.99 with the code, it's $100 less than the Effortless and has the same 15-year warranty. It comes in red and silver at the same price.

JC4000: the cheapest Omega slow juicer

The JC4000 has a 3-inch mouth that fits whole fruit, two speeds, a reverse function for clogs, a digital touch pad and dishwasher-safe parts. It's the lightest of the five at 8.16 lb and has a 120-watt motor. Buyers rate it 4.4 from 52 reviews.

At $134.99, or $121.49 with the code, it costs about a third of the Pure Press. The catch is the 12-month warranty. It suits someone trying a slow juicer for the first time, or a small kitchen.

Time Saving batch juicer: batch juicing for less

The Time Saving OMJCSMVB works like the Effortless, with a hopper for a whole recipe and no mesh filter, at a third of the price: $159.99, down from $199.99, or $143.99 with the code. It runs at 55 RPM on 150 watts and weighs 12.83 lb. It comes with a juice container, pulp container, pusher and brush.

What you give up compared with the Effortless: Omega doesn't list a 2-liter hopper or an auto-stop timer for it, and the warranty is 1 year instead of 15. Its rating is 4.2 from 75 reviews. The black and red versions were in stock when we checked.

High Speed juicer: fastest and cheapest

The High Speed OMJCHSXLC is the only centrifugal juicer in the range: a 1,000-watt motor spinning at 12,500 RPM, a 3.3-inch chute, two speeds, a stainless-steel filter and a 9-cup pulp container. Omega pitches it for juice "in seconds." It has a 12-month warranty and no store rating was shown.

At $109.99, or $98.99 with the code, it's the lowest price in the range. Choose it if speed matters more to you than a slow juicer's design, and you mostly juice fruit and firm vegetables.
