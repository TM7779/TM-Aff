Choose the TH80 V2 to save money

The TH80 V2 is the cheaper way into the same typing base. Epomaker builds it with a soft gasket between the plate and the case, a flex-cut PC plate and PCB, and layers of PORON, IXPE, latex and silicone foam, which it says give the deep, creamy sound the series is known for. The PCB takes 3- and 5-pin switches, so you can swap the stock switches later without soldering.

It connects to up to five devices over Bluetooth, the 2.4GHz dongle (stored under the kickstand) or a USB-C cable, and the 8,000mAh battery is rated for 200 hours with the lights off. It also has an RGB light strip along the side. Epomaker's own reviews are mostly positive about value and sound, but two of the 17 mention keys registering twice after some use, so keep the box during the 30-day return window.

Choose the TH80 V2 Pro for the screen and bigger battery

The V2 Pro keeps the same footprint and adds three things. The 1.06-inch glass screen shows the time, battery level and keyboard status, and you can upload your own GIFs through Epomaker's web software, so there's nothing to install. The battery grows to 10,000mAh. And the metal knob, which controls volume or the screen, is a swappable module that you can replace with a standard key.

Epomaker lists PBT keycaps in a Cherry-style profile with Mac legends, and a pink version that the V2 doesn't come in. It's slightly heavier (941 g) and 2 mm taller at the back. With 32 reviews on Epomaker's site, all four or five stars, it's also the better reviewed of the two there. On sale it cost $7.65 more than the V2 with the same Creamy Jade switches.

Creamy Jade or Sea Salt Silent?

Both keyboards come with the same two switch options. Epomaker describes the Creamy Jade as a crisp, responsive switch for typing and gaming, and the Sea Salt Silent V2 as the quiet option for offices and shared rooms. The silent version costs 85 cents more on the V2 and $1.70 more on the V2 Pro. If you aren't sure, remember both boards are hot-swappable, so you can change switches later.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between the Epomaker TH80 V2 and V2 Pro?

The V2 Pro adds a 1.06-inch glass screen, a bigger 10,000mAh battery (against 8,000mAh) and a swappable metal knob, and comes in pink as well as black and white. It weighs 941 g against 0.9 kg and costs about $8 more on sale.

Is the Epomaker TH80 V2 Pro worth it over the V2?

If you want the screen for time, battery and GIFs, or the larger battery for long wireless use with the lights on, yes: it was $66.29 against $58.64 on sale. If not, the V2 types on the same gasket-mounted, hot-swap base for less.

How long does the Epomaker TH80 V2 battery last?

Epomaker lists 200 hours with the RGB off and 40 hours with it on, from an 8,000mAh battery. For the V2 Pro it lists around 40 to 200 hours from 10,000mAh, depending on the screen and backlight.

Is the Epomaker TH80 V2 wireless?

Yes. Both the TH80 V2 and V2 Pro work over Bluetooth 5.0, a 2.4GHz dongle and a USB-C cable, at 1000Hz wired or on 2.4GHz and 125Hz on Bluetooth, according to Epomaker.

Is the Epomaker TH80 V2 hot-swappable?

Yes. Both have a 3/5-pin hot-swap PCB, so most MX-style switches fit without soldering, and a switch and keycap puller comes in the box.

Which switch should I pick for the TH80 V2?

Epomaker offers two: the Creamy Jade, which it describes as a crisp, responsive switch for typing and gaming, and the Sea Salt Silent V2, a quieter switch for shared spaces. The silent version cost about 85 cents more on sale.

Does the Epomaker TH80 V2 work with Mac?

Yes. Epomaker lists Mac, Windows and Android support, and both models include Mac modifier keycaps.

Is the Epomaker TH80 V2 a 75% or 80% keyboard?

It's a 75% layout despite the 80 in its name: a function row and arrow keys, but no number pad, in a 323 mm wide case.
