Choose the X4 for everyday carry

The X4 is Wuben's most reviewed light on its own site, with 471 reviews, and the cheapest of the four. It's a flat, 95.7 mm body that weighs 141 g with its battery, so it sits in a jeans pocket like a phone rather than a tube. The main light turns from 5 up to 1,500 lumens with a rotary dial and reaches 205 m; Wuben lists 3 hours at full power and 285 hours at the lowest setting.

The side light is what sets it apart: a white panel (1 to 100 lumens), an RGB light and red-blue, red-green and red-yellow flashing modes, which Wuben lists for signaling and being seen on a road or trail. A magnet and a clip let you stick it to a car or a fridge. The battery is a standard 18650 (3,400mAh in the box) that charges over USB-C and can be swapped for a spare.

Choose the X2 Pro+ if you want AA batteries as a backup

The X2 Pro+ matches the X4 on paper, 1,500 lumens and 205 m, with stepless dimming. The difference is power: it comes with two 920mAh 14500 lithium cells, can run on just one, and also takes standard AA batteries, which Wuben says gives up to 180 hours on eco. If you keep a light in a car, a go-bag or a drawer for power cuts, being able to drop in AAs from any shop is the reason to choose it.

It has a red light as well as white, a strong magnet for hands-free use, an aluminum body and IP65. At $39.99 it costs about $3 more than the X4 in grey or white, and it has far fewer reviews on Wuben's site (23). Wuben's page doesn't give its weight or size in text.

Choose the X5 for UV and a laser pointer

The X5 is a 3-in-1: a 1,300-lumen white light with a 118 m beam, a 2W 365 nm UV light and a green laser on its own button. Wuben lists UV for inspection work and the laser for pointing. It's the slimmest of the four at 21 mm thick and 128 g, with a 1,200mAh replaceable battery that charges over USB-C.

Wuben sells two laser versions at the same $63.99: a Class 3R (5 mW) and a Class 1. The lower class is the safer choice around children; never point any laser at people, animals, cars or aircraft. If you don't need UV or a laser, the X4 gives more light and a longer beam for less.

See the X5 at WUBEN

Choose the X1 Pro for maximum light and a power bank

The X1 Pro is in another class: up to 12,300 lumens from combined flood and spot beams, 410 m of throw and 42,025 candela, Wuben says. Full power lasts about a minute before it steps down; at 3,000 lumens it lists 1.8 hours, and at 20 lumens 220 hours. Two 4,800mAh 21700 cells (9,600mAh) charge at 30W over USB-C, and it can charge your phone at 15W, so it doubles as a power bank. Smart cooling keeps the output steady, and a fan module is sold as an add-on.

The price of all that is size: 138 mm long, almost 60 mm wide and 383 g, about two and a half X4s. It's a light for a backpack, a truck or search work rather than a pocket. At $111.99 it was about $63 off its listed $174.99.

Questions people ask

Which Wuben flashlight is best?

For everyday carry, the X4: 1,500 lumens, a 205 m beam, a swappable 18650 battery and a magnet for $37.04, and it's the most reviewed light on wubenlight.com. For the most light, the X1 Pro at 12,300 lumens, but it weighs 383 g.

What is the difference between the Wuben X1 Pro and X4?

The X1 Pro is far brighter (12,300 vs 1,500 lumens) and throws twice as far (410 vs 205 m), and it works as a 15W power bank. The X4 is much smaller and lighter, 141 g against 383 g, and costs $37.04 against $111.99.

Wuben X2 Pro+ vs X4: which should I get?

Both reach 1,500 lumens and 205 m. Get the X2 Pro+ ($39.99) if you want it to run on AA batteries in a pinch; get the X4 ($37.04 to $38.99) for its RGB side light, rotary dimming and an 18650 battery.

Is there a Wuben X4 Pro?

We didn't see an X4 Pro on wubenlight.com on November 24, 2026. The X4 there is sold in grey, white, black and a Christmas Limited Edition ($49.99).

How long does the Wuben X4 battery last?

Wuben lists 3 hours for the main light at 1,500 lumens, up to 285 hours at its lowest setting, and up to 720 hours for the side light at its lowest, using its 3,400mAh 18650 battery.

Can the Wuben X1 Pro charge a phone?

Yes. Wuben says it gives 15W reverse charging from its 9,600mAh battery pack, and it recharges itself at 30W over USB-C.

Does the Wuben X2 Pro+ take AA batteries?

Yes. It ships with two 920mAh 14500 lithium batteries and also takes standard AA batteries, Wuben says, with up to 180 hours on eco.

What laser class is the Wuben X5?

Wuben sells two versions at $63.99: a Class 3R version with a 5 mW green laser and a Class 1 version. Never point either at people, animals, vehicles or aircraft.

Are Wuben flashlights waterproof?

All four lights here are rated IP65, which means protection against dust and water jets, not submersion, and Wuben lists a 1 m drop rating for each.
