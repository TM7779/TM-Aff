GRECELL 300W vs 1000W vs 2400W: Which Power Station Should You Buy?

For charging phones, laptops and lights on a camping trip, the GRECELL 300W (T300S, $179.99) is enough. To keep a small fridge or several devices going through a power cut, the 1000W (T-1000, $359.99 in green) gives you more than four times the battery for twice the price. Buy the 2400W ($549.99) only if you need to run 1,000 to 2,400-watt appliances like a microwave or space heater, or want a LiFePO4 battery rated for 3,500+ cycles. When we checked, the 1000W cost less than the 500W, so skip the 500W unless its price drops.

How long will each one run your devices?

GRECELL's own rule of thumb, printed on its 1000W page, is: running time = capacity in Wh × 0.85 ÷ the watts your device uses. The 0.85 covers what's lost turning battery power into AC. Using that formula:

Choose the 240W if you only charge by USB

The 240W is really a big power bank: 153.6Wh, a 100W USB-C port that can charge most laptops, a second 30W USB-C, a USB-A port, a 12V DC outlet and a light with an SOS mode. Its spec table lists no AC outlet, so it won't run anything with a wall plug. It uses a LiFePO4 battery and recharges in 1.5 to 2 hours from a 100W input, says GRECELL. At $99.99 it's the cheapest way into the range.

Choose the 300W for camping and travel

The T300S adds a 330W AC outlet (600W peak) to 230.8Wh of battery, plus a 60W USB-C, an 18W USB-C, USB-A ports and a 12V car outlet, at 10.2 lbs. That covers a laptop, a camera, lights, or a small fan through a night or two of camping, going by the formula above.

The catch is charging: its input tops out at 45W from the wall, the car or a solar panel, so a full recharge takes hours. It uses an NCM lithium-ion battery rather than LiFePO4.

Choose the 1000W for power cuts

The T-1000 has 999Wh, two AC outlets with 1000W total (2000W peak), a 60W USB-C, three USB-A ports, two 12V DC outlets, a car outlet and a 10W wireless pad, at 17.9 lbs. Choose the 2400W for big appliances

The H2400 is a different class: 1843.2Wh, four AC outlets with 2400W total (4800W surge), two 100W USB-C ports, and a LiFePO4 battery GRECELL rates for 3,500+ cycles. GRECELL lists a microwave, coffee maker, space heater or window air conditioner (up to 2400W) among the things it can run, and says it recharges from a wall outlet in 1.6 to 2 hours at up to 1200W. It also has a UPS mode that GRECELL says switches over within 10ms for devices under 1800W.

At 55 lbs, it's a home-backup unit you wheel or carry with two hands, not a camping box. At $549.99 it had the lowest price per Wh in the range.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between the GRECELL 300W and 1000W?

The 1000W (T-1000) has 999Wh against the 300W's 230.8Wh, two AC outlets with 1000W total instead of one 330W outlet, and a 155W input instead of 45W. It weighs 17.9 lbs to the 300W's 10.2 lbs, and cost $359.99 in green against $179.99 when we checked.

Which GRECELL power station is best?

The 300W for camping and charging devices, the 1000W for power cuts and a small fridge, and the 2400W for big appliances. On November 22, 2026 the 1000W was cheaper than the 500W, so it was the better buy of the two.

How long does the GRECELL 1000W last?

By GRECELL's own formula (999Wh × 0.85 ÷ device watts), about 8.5 hours on a 100-watt device and about 14 hours on a 60-watt laptop. Real times vary with the device.

Can the GRECELL 1000W run a refrigerator?

Only one that draws under its 1000W AC output, including the start-up surge (2000W peak). GRECELL says not to power devices rated above 1000 watts; check your fridge's label for its watts.

Does the GRECELL 240W have an AC outlet?

No. Its spec table lists USB-C (100W and 30W), USB-A and a 12V DC outlet, but no AC outlet.

What battery does GRECELL use?

The 240W and 2400W use LiFePO4 batteries; GRECELL rates the 2400W for 3,500+ cycles. The 300W (T300S), 500W and 1000W use NCM lithium-ion batteries.
