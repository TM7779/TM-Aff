The short answer

SEEQ is a real US brand from Minneapolis with thousands of reviews on its own store, 4.7 to 4.8 out of 5 per flavor. It costs $2.45 a serving for a tub, or less with the code or a subscription. Two things to know: everything is final sale, and it has almost no independent reviews on Trustpilot yet.

What SEEQ sells

SEEQ sells one product in several flavors: Clear Protein in Blue Razz Freeze, Mango Pineapple, Strawberry Lemonade, Fruit Punch and a limited Juicy Peach, at $54 for a 22-serving tub. A $49 variety pack holds 18 single-serve packets in three flavors.

The product page lists 22 g of protein, 100 calories, 0 g sugar and 0 g lactose per serving, made with whey protein isolate and sweetened with monk fruit. Whey comes from milk; talk to a doctor first if you're pregnant or take medication.

Is SEEQ legit?

Yes, by what we could check. The store account lists Minneapolis, Minnesota, it publishes its shipping and return rules, and its products have thousands of ratings. People also search for SEEQ at Walmart and in Canada, but we only checked SEEQ's own store.

Is it worth the money?

At $2.45 a serving for a tub, or $1.96 on subscription by the product page, SEEQ costs more than a basic whey powder and is in line with other clear whey isolates. You're paying for the light, juice-like drink rather than more protein per scoop.
