The short answer

Shaka Guide is a legitimate Hawaii company that has sold self-guided audio tours since 2016. Its tours play stories, music and directions by GPS as you drive or walk, and work offline once downloaded. Reviewers mostly love the storytelling; the complaints are about narration running ahead of or behind where you are, closed stops, and some tours feeling more like directions than history.

What Shaka Guide sells

The app is free. You buy tours, one at a time or in bundles, on shakaguide.com or in the app. The site listed 106 tours on November 22, 2026: 21 in Hawaii, 55 in national parks, 36 scenic drives and 17 walking tours (some count in more than one group).

Of the 100 most recent reviews of the main app and the Maui app on the App Store, 90 were five stars. Reviewers praise the stories and music, directions that keep working with no cell signal, and being able to stop wherever they like instead of following a bus tour.

The complaints are worth knowing. One Big Island bundle buyer found it too focused on directions and short on history. Others said the narration played ahead of where they were, cut off and picked back up confusingly, or that some stops were closed. One Maui reviewer said they missed the waterfalls they hoped to see. Most of these issues are easier to avoid if you download the tour over Wi-Fi first and start it at the suggested starting point.

Is Shaka Guide legit?

Yes. Shaka Guide LLC is the seller named on its App Store apps, says it opened in 2016 and is rooted in Hawaii, and names its team, including its two co-founders, on its About page. It lists a support phone number with office hours in Hawaii time, a chat and an email address, and publishes its terms, privacy policy and an FAQ on downloads and refunds. Purchases go through a normal checkout, and your receipt carries a redeem code for the app.

Is it worth the money?

For one big drive, a single tour of $15 to $25 costs less than a guided bus seat for one person, and covers the whole car. If you'll take two tours or more on one island, the $34.99 bundle is the better deal: the Maui bundle's six tours cost $124.94 bought one by one.

Who it's for

Shaka Guide suits families and couples renting a car in Hawaii or visiting national parks who want a guide's stories without a fixed schedule. It suits less those who want a live guide to answer questions, those relying on Android Auto, or anyone who won't have Wi-Fi to download the tour before setting out.

Is Shaka Guide legit?

It works with Apple CarPlay. Its FAQ says it doesn't work with Android Auto yet, so Android users play it from the phone or through the car stereo by cable or Bluetooth.
