The short answer

Wuben is a real flashlight brand whose lights are rated well on its own store, 4.56 to 4.91 out of 5. Its Trustpilot score is lower, 4.0 from 423 reviews, and most of the 1-star reviews are about orders that were slow to arrive or never came, not about the lights. Prices are low for small lights, from $18.39, and the warranty runs 5 years for registered products.

What Wuben sells

The best sellers when we checked were the E2 PULSE keychain light ($19.97), the G5 400-lumen keychain light ($18.39), the X1 Pro 12,300-lumen EDC light ($111.99), the L3 3,000-lumen tactical pocket light ($39.99), the E19 high-CRI pen light ($19.97) and the A1 20,000-lumen spotlight ($728.99). Most were on sale below a higher listed price.

On Trustpilot, 277 of the 423 reviews give 5 stars and 103 give 1 star. Fans praise bright, compact lights such as the E2, E7, G5 and X4, and helpful support.

The 1-star reviews mostly describe shipping: orders that took weeks, incomplete tracking, or lights that never arrived, with one reviewer calling it a dropshipping service. A few mention build issues, such as a mode selector that broke. The store's own ratings are higher than Trustpilot's, as is common.

Is Wuben legit?

Yes, by what we could check. Its Trustpilot profile lists an address in Palatine, Illinois, and the store's account lists Mong Kok, Hong Kong. It publishes its shipping, return and warranty policies, and ships from warehouses in the US and Germany as well as China, which explains why delivery times vary so much.

Is it worth the money?

For small lights, yes: an E2 PULSE or G5 costs under $20 on sale, and the 5-year repair warranty for registered products is longer than many budget brands offer. If you need the light by a set date, check at checkout whether it ships from an overseas warehouse (3 to 7 business days to North America) or from China (12 to 28).
