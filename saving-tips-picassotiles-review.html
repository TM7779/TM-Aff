The short answer

PicassoTiles is a real toy brand that has sold magnetic tiles for about a decade, by its own account, and is stocked by retailers such as Target, Walmart and Michaels. On its own store, the Classic Starter Set of 100 pieces is rated 4.98 out of 5 from 60 reviews. The bigger race track set scores lower, at 4.20 from just 5 reviews. Returns are 30 days, unopened only, and it ships to the 48 contiguous US states only.

What PicassoTiles sells

Classic see-through magnetic tiles in sets from 60 to 100 pieces, race track sets with battery-powered LED cars, magnetic marble runs, small travel sets in tins, whiteboard tiles with markers, clip-on windows and doors, and bundles that put several sets together. The sets are labeled for ages 3 and up and come without instructions, which the brand says is on purpose, to leave room for open-ended play.

When we checked, the 60-piece set cost $24.99, the 100-piece set $39.99, the 108-piece marble run $59.99 and the 150-piece race track with 4 LED cars $139.98.

Reviewers on the store mostly write about how long children play with the sets and how they build bigger layouts over time; several buyers say they came back for more sets. The main complaint we found is on the 150-piece race track: one parent wrote that the posts for a second level didn't line up and the track came apart, and the brand replied asking for the batch number and photos. These are reviews collected by the store itself, and the larger sets have only a handful of them.

Is PicassoTiles legit?

Yes. picassotiles.com publishes its shipping and refund policies, answers at a support email, and lists the US retailers that carry it, including Target, Walmart, Best Buy, Michaels, Kohl's, Staples and BJ's. If you buy from one of those, ask that retailer about returns: PicassoTiles sends buyers from other stores back to the seller, and says sets from unauthorized resellers don't qualify for a warranty replacement.

Is it worth the money?

At $39.99 for 100 pieces, the starter set works out to about 40 cents a tile, and it's the best-reviewed set on the store. The track and marble run sets cost more per piece because they include cars, ramps and tubes. Orders of $69 or more ship free, and the Sibling Builder bundle costs $5.72 less than its three sets bought apart. Since only unopened sets can go back, start with a smaller set if you're unsure.
