Simpletics review: the short answer

Simpletics is a real brand with a Shopify store, full policies and a 30-day money-back guarantee that covers opened products. Its Sea Salt Spray scores 4.3 out of 5 from 872 reviews on its own store, and the Texture Powder 4.3 from 215. But Trustpilot shows a TrustScore of 2 from 22 reviews, most of them about orders that arrived late or not at all outside the US, unexpected subscriptions and slow replies. Buy with a card or PayPal, pick the one-time purchase option, and keep your order emails.

What Simpletics sells

Four styling products, each with a short ingredient list printed on its page: Sea Salt Spray (8 oz, $23.97; water, Pink Himalayan salt, gum arabic, essential oil and potassium sorbate), Hair Clay ($24.97; bentonite clay, beeswax, coconut and castor oil), Texture Powder (4 g, $22.97) and Curl Mousse (6 fl oz, $22.97). It also sells duos and kits of these, a sandalwood comb, a silicone scalp scrubber, a face towel and a valet tray.

Its About page says the products are made in the USA in a GMP-certified facility and that the brand has more than 300,000 customers. Those are the brand's own figures.

Is Simpletics legit?

It's a real, operating brand: it sells through a Shopify checkout, publishes shipping, refund and contact pages, and lists a US return address in Lutz, Florida. The fair worry isn't whether it exists but how it handles problems, given the Trustpilot complaints. Paying by card or PayPal gives you buyer protection, and the refund policy asks you to email support@simpletics.com within 30 days of delivery.

Is Simpletics worth the money?

At $23.97 for 8 oz, the Sea Salt Spray costs about $3 an ounce, in the range of other direct-to-consumer styling sprays. The kits are where the value is: the Full Styling Kit is $57.53 against $71.91 for its three products bought alone. The 30-day guarantee on opened products lowers the risk of trying it, as long as you get a reply.
