Choose the V3 for a simple stereo system

If your source has RCA outputs and you have two passive speakers, the V3 does the job for the least money. It's the smallest of the three, and its pre-out can feed a powered subwoofer or another amp, with the volume knob controlling that output too.

You give up balanced inputs and the mono option. Whether that matters depends on your gear: if nothing you own has XLR outputs, you won't use them.

Choose the ZA3 for XLR or a subwoofer

The ZA3 is the one to buy when you want extra connections in one box: XLR and ¼" TRS inputs, a subwoofer output, a 12V trigger that switches it on with your other gear, and five swappable op-amps instead of the V3's two. It's also larger, with side vents that Fosi says give better cooling.

You pay $44 more than the V3 with the same power supply, and more features don't automatically mean better sound. TNT-Audio's May 2024 review compared the two and preferred the simpler V3, finding it slightly better. It also dismissed the filtered sub output and the balanced inputs as pointless at this price. That's one reviewer's listening test, so treat it as an opinion, but it's a good reason not to buy the ZA3 for sound alone. The store ratings of the two are close.

Choose the V3 Mono for a pair of monoblocks

The V3 Mono drives one speaker, so a stereo system needs two. Fosi uses PFFB (post-filter feedback) in this model and says it reduces the way Class-D amps react to different speakers. On paper it has the best noise figure of the three, at 123dB, and the best SINAD when you use XLR.

It suits people who already have a preamp or DAC with volume control. The Mono has an on, auto and off switch and an XLR or RCA switch on the front, but no volume knob appears in Fosi's photos, so check your setup first. Fosi also suggests it for adding power to speakers on a multi-channel AV receiver.

What all three share

All three use the same TI TPA3255 chip, handle speakers from 2 to 8Ω and let you swap op-amps. Fosi's spec lists show no Bluetooth, remote or built-in DAC on any of them, so you'll need a source with a line output. Questions people ask

Is the Fosi Audio ZA3 better than the V3?

It has more connections, not clearly better sound. The ZA3 adds XLR and ¼" TRS inputs, a sub output, a 12V trigger and a mono switch. Fosi lists the V3 with lower distortion (under 0.003% against under 0.006%) and a higher signal-to-noise ratio (110dB against 106dB), and TNT-Audio's reviewer preferred the V3's sound.

What's the difference between the Fosi V3 and V3 Mono?

The V3 is a stereo amp that drives two speakers. The V3 Mono drives one speaker, so stereo takes two of them. The Mono adds XLR and ¼" TRS inputs and PFFB feedback, and Fosi lists it at 123dB signal-to-noise against 110dB for the V3.

Do I need two V3 Mono amps for stereo?

Yes. Each one drives a single speaker. Fosi sells two with one shared 48V/10A power supply for $279.99.

Can the ZA3 run in mono?

Yes. A switch changes it from stereo to mono, and Fosi rates it at 235W into 4Ω in mono. You need two for a stereo pair.

V3 Mono or two ZA3s in mono mode?

The V3 Mono pair costs less: $279.99 against $299.98 for two ZA3s with 48V supplies. Fosi also rates the Mono slightly higher, at 240W against 235W into 4Ω, and lists a higher signal-to-noise ratio.

Which power supply should I get, 32V or 48V?

48V if your speakers are hard to drive or you listen loud. Fosi rates the V3 Mono at 240W into 4Ω on 48V and only 100W on 32V. The 48V version of the V3 or ZA3 costs $20 more than the 32V one.

Does the Fosi V3 have XLR inputs?

No. The V3 has RCA inputs only. The ZA3 and V3 Mono have RCA, XLR and ¼" TRS.

Do the V3 and ZA3 have Bluetooth or a remote?

Fosi's spec lists show neither, and no built-in DAC either. You need a source with a line output, such as a CD player, a streamer or a DAC. The BT20A Pro is Fosi's Bluetooth model in this range.
