Choose regular tiles for a first set

Regular PicassoTiles are the full-size tiles most people mean when they say "magnetic tiles." The Classic 60-piece set lists exactly what's in the box: 4 large squares (6 by 6 inches), 24 small squares (3 by 3 inches), 18 small triangles, 8 right triangles (3 by 4 inches) and 6 tall triangles (3 by 5.5 inches).

At $24.99 it was the same price as the 36-piece starter set when we checked, so it gives you 24 more pieces for the money: about $0.42 a piece against $0.69. It had a 5.0 rating from 24 reviews on picassotiles.com. Because themed full-size sets and Nano tiles connect to it, it's the set to start with if you plan to add more later.

Choose Mini Diamond as a separate travel set

Mini Diamond tiles are travel-sized tiles with a shimmering, diamond-textured surface. The important line is on PicassoTiles' own product page for the 61-piece set: they work with other Mini Diamond and travel sets, but they are not compatible with the regular full-size tiles. So a Mini Diamond set is a second, separate toy, not an add-on to a full-size collection.

The 100-piece set ($34.99) comes with 38 squares, 30 equilateral triangles, 20 right triangles, 9 isosceles triangles and 3 large squares, and had a 4.8 rating from 5 reviews. The 61-piece set was only $13.65 when we checked, about $0.22 a piece, the lowest per-piece price in this guide. PicassoTiles doesn't list the Mini tile size in inches.

Choose Nano tiles for travel that still fits

Nano tiles are PicassoTiles' other small size, sold in travel tins. Unlike the Mini Diamond line, PicassoTiles says Nano tiles are compatible with its full-size playsets, so a child can take a tin on a trip and then add the pieces to the big set at home.

The 50-piece tin ($16.19) has 39 triangles in several sizes and 11 squares, and PicassoTiles lists the tin at 7.6 by 4.8 by 1.2 inches. The tin is magnetic, so it doubles as a building surface. The 40-piece tin is $14.57.

What age are PicassoTiles for?

Every set in this guide is labeled for ages 3 and up on picassotiles.com: the Classic and starter sets, the Mini Diamond 100-piece set and the Nano tins. PicassoTiles doesn't label them for younger children, so for a 2-year-old, follow the age on the box. Within ages 3 and up, size is less about age than about how you'll use the tiles: full-size for big builds at home, Nano or Mini for small hands on the go.

Questions people ask

Are PicassoTiles Mini tiles compatible with regular PicassoTiles?

No. PicassoTiles says its Mini Diamond sets connect with other Mini Diamond and travel sets, but are not compatible with its regular full-size magnetic tiles.

Are PicassoTiles Nano tiles compatible with regular PicassoTiles?

Yes, by PicassoTiles' own description: its Nano travel sets are compatible with full-size PicassoTiles playsets and with other Nano sets.

What age are PicassoTiles for?

PicassoTiles labels the sets in this guide for ages 3 and up, including the Classic 60-piece set, the Mini Diamond 100-piece set and the Nano travel tins.

Are PicassoTiles OK for a 2-year-old?

PicassoTiles labels these sets for ages 3 and up, not for 2-year-olds. Follow the age on the box of the set you buy.

How big are regular PicassoTiles?

In the Classic 60-piece set, the small squares are 3 by 3 inches and the large squares are 6 by 6 inches, with triangles from 3 by 3 to 3 by 5.5 inches.

How big are PicassoTiles Mini tiles?

PicassoTiles' Mini Diamond product pages don't list the tile size in inches. They describe them as travel-sized tiles with a diamond-textured surface.

Which PicassoTiles set is best for a first set?

The Classic 60-piece set ($24.99) is the cheapest regular-size set we found with large squares included, and later full-size and Nano sets connect to it. It had a 5.0 rating from 24 reviews on picassotiles.com.

Which PicassoTiles set is best for travel?

A Nano tin, such as the 40-piece set ($14.57) or the 50-piece set ($16.19): the tin stores the tiles and doubles as a building surface, and Nano tiles also connect to full-size PicassoTiles.
